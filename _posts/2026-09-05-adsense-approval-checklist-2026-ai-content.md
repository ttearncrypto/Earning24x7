If you've applied for Google AdSense recently and gotten the vague "low value content" rejection, you're not alone, and you're probably not doing anything wildly wrong either. Approval has gotten tougher over the past couple of years, and the rise of AI writing tools has made Google's reviewers more careful, not less.

Google has never actually banned AI generated content from AdSense. What it penalizes is thin, generic, unedited content, regardless of whether a human or a tool wrote it. Most of the confusion comes from companies mixing up "AI content is risky" with the real issue: "low effort, unedited content is risky."

If you run a business website, a startup blog, or a local service site and you're using AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude to help you write, this checklist walks through exactly what to fix before you apply, so you're not left guessing why your site got rejected for the third time.

## Does Google Actually Penalize AI Content in 2026?

No, not directly. Google's own [Search Central documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} has said for a while now that its focus is on the quality of content, not the tool used to produce it. That policy hasn't changed. What has changed is enforcement around what Google calls scaled content abuse, which is when a site publishes large volumes of thin, templated content with little to no original value, often to manipulate rankings quickly.

A March 2026 update strengthened enforcement of this exact pattern. Sites publishing dozens or hundreds of near identical AI articles daily, often built around a "[service] in [city]" template with almost nothing changed between pages, got hit hardest. Meanwhile, sites publishing a smaller number of well edited, useful AI assisted articles have continued to rank and get approved.

The pattern that shows up across multiple 2026 case studies is consistent. Sites publishing 50 to 100 quality AI assisted articles with human editing tend to see solid traffic growth, while sites dumping a thousand or more unedited AI articles tend to see sharp drops. The editorial process around the content is what separates the two outcomes.

### What This Means for Your Business Site

If you're a business owner using AI to help draft blog content, service pages, or FAQs, you're not doing anything against policy. The risk only shows up if you're publishing AI output straight from the tool with no fact checking, no local context, and no personal expertise added. For most small business sites, that's an easy fix, not a dealbreaker.

Here's a full breakdown of what actually matters, organized so you can work through it in order. The categories that trip businesses up the most come first.

## Step by Step: What to Fix Before You Apply

### 1. Publish Enough Useful Content

Most current guidance points to somewhere between 15 and 30 solid articles or pages before applying, each running roughly 800 to 1,200 words with real depth. There's no official Google published number, but this range shows up consistently across sites that get approved on the first try. Google's own [helpful content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} expects pages to serve the person reading them first, and that bar is the same one AdSense reviewers apply.

**Actionable tip:** Before applying, list your published pages and honestly ask which ones you'd bookmark or share yourself. If the answer is none, that's your priority list to fix first.

### 2. Fix Your AI Content Before It Becomes a Liability

If you're using AI tools to draft content, don't publish the raw output. Here's a simple three step process that keeps AI content compliant:

* **Add original insight.** Include something the AI couldn't know, like a specific example from your business, a local detail about your city or industry, or a number from your own experience.
* **Fact check everything.** AI tools occasionally get statistics, dates, or technical details wrong. A single confidently wrong fact can undercut the trust signal your whole site is trying to build.
* **Edit for voice.** Read it out loud. If it sounds like it could be copy pasted onto any competitor's site with just the business name swapped, it needs more of your actual perspective in it.

**Actionable tip:** A simple, honest line in your author bio like "researched with AI assistance and reviewed by the site owner" is not currently required by AdSense, but it's an honest trust signal that tends to help rather than hurt.

### 3. Get Your Essential Pages in Order

This is one of the most common reasons for rejection, and it has nothing to do with AI content at all. Make sure every page listed below exists, is useful rather than generic template copy, and is linked from your site's footer so it's reachable from any page. The whole site also has to stay inside the [AdSense Program Policies](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/48182?hl=en){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, which cover everything from copyrighted material to how ads are placed.

* Privacy Policy
* About Us page with real information about who runs the business
* Contact page with a working email or contact form
* Terms and Conditions

**Actionable tip:** Don't just copy a generic privacy policy template word for word from another site. Update it with your actual business details and how you specifically handle data.

### 4. Confirm Your Site Passes Basic Technical Checks

These are largely binary, meaning you either pass or you don't, and they're checked automatically before a human reviewer even looks at your content.

* Your entire site must run on [HTTPS](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724?hl=en){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, with no leftover HTTP resources anywhere
* Your site must pass Google's [Mobile Friendly Test](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2016/05/a-new-mobile-friendly-testing-tool){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} cleanly
* Your site must be fully live, not under construction or password protected
* You need direct access to your site's HTML to place the AdSense verification code

**Actionable tip:** Run your homepage and two or three inner pages through Google's Mobile Friendly Test before applying. It takes two minutes and catches issues you might not notice on your own phone.

### 5. Understand the Domain Age Situation

Outside of India and China, Google doesn't publish a strict minimum domain age requirement. Still, most sites that get approved smoothly tend to be at least a few months old, simply because that's roughly how long it takes to build a real content library and get indexed properly. If you're in India or China, budgeting for around six months of site history before applying is the safer approach.

**Actionable tip:** Use the waiting period productively. Instead of just letting the domain age, keep publishing consistently so you have both time and content depth working in your favor when you do apply.

### 6. Avoid the Scaled Content Trap

If you're tempted to publish a large batch of AI generated location pages, like "[your service] in [city]" repeated across fifty different towns with only the city name changed, this is exactly the pattern Google's 2026 enforcement update targeted. Pages like this, with no real local insight or original value, tend to get flagged as scaled content abuse rather than genuine local SEO.

**Actionable tip:** If you're creating city or location specific pages, make each one different in a real way, with local details, testimonials, or service specifics for that area rather than a swapped variable.

## Common Rejection Reasons and How to Read Them

| Rejection Message | What It Usually Means | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Low value content | Content is too generic, thin, or reads like it could be on any site | Add depth, original insight, and edit AI drafts thoroughly |
| Site under construction | Missing pages or placeholder content found during crawl | Fully complete and publish every page before reapplying |
| Site cannot be accessed | Crawler couldn't reach your site | Check robots.txt and confirm the site isn't password protected |
| Insufficient content | Too few published pages at time of application | Publish a larger library before you apply again, and fix the gaps that caused the rejection |

## Key Takeaways

* Google does not ban AI generated content from AdSense. It penalizes thin, unedited, low value content, whether written by a human or a tool.
* Aim for 15 to 30 useful, well developed articles or pages before applying. A published page that serves a real question beats filler volume every time.
* AI drafts should always be edited for accuracy, original insight, and voice before publishing, not used as final copy.
* Essential pages like Privacy Policy, About, Contact, and Terms need to be complete, accurate, and easy to find from every page.
* HTTPS, mobile friendliness, and full site accessibility are binary technical requirements that can sink an application before a human reviewer sees your content.
* Avoid publishing large batches of near identical location or template pages, since this pattern is specifically targeted by scaled content abuse enforcement.
* There's no strict domain age requirement outside India and China, but most smoothly approved sites are a few months old simply because that's how long it takes to build real depth.
