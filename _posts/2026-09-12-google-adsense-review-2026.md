## What AdSense Is Like for Publishers

AdSense is a set it and forget it network in the best way. You add a few lines of code, Google handles the ad auctions, and you check a dashboard each week. That simplicity is the real appeal, and it's why most people start here.

### The money side

The revenue share is the headline number. Google's [official revenue share page](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/180195){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} states publishers keep 80% of AdSense for Content revenue after the advertiser platform takes its fee. When those ads are bought through Google Ads, the buy side keeps about 15%, so publishers end up netting roughly 68%. That is honest and on par with many networks, but below the 75% to 90% premium networks advertise to larger sites.

Payments are monthly and dependable. Details on [payment thresholds](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1709871){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} show the standard payout floor is $100 USD or local equivalent. Your balance first needs to pass a $10 verification threshold and a $10 payment method selection threshold, then once you cross $100 by the end of a month with no holds, payment lands between the 21st and 26th. [Google's steps to getting paid page](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1709858){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} walks through identity verification, address PIN verification, and bank details in that order.

Payment options keep growing. As of 2026, US publishers can also receive earnings through PayPal Hyperwallet, PayPal, Venmo, or cash pickup. Earlier in 2026 Google retired euro denominated cheques, so if you're in Europe, EFT, SEPA, or wire transfer are the realistic paths.

### What drags it down

The approval process is strict and can feel random. There's no traffic minimum on paper, but sites with thin content or missing Privacy and About pages get bounced quickly, and the rejection reasons can be vague. We dug into the exact fixes in our [AdSense approval checklist for 2026]({{ '/2026/09/05/adsense-approval-checklist-2026-ai-content.html' | relative_url }}).

Beyond approval, expect two realities. Small and niche sites get low fill and low CPMs because Google's demand is broad rather than niche specific. And you get zero direct control over which advertisers appear, only blocklists you maintain yourself. Pair that with growing ad blocker adoption and revenue from casual content sites keeps shrinking, while niche authority sites outgrow their 80% share quickly.

## What AdSense Means for Advertisers

AdSense sits on the sell side. Publishers add the code, Google auctions the space, and advertisers buy that inventory through Google Ads, the Display & Video 360, or third party demand platforms.

For advertisers, the draw is reach and relevance. AdSense inventory spans millions of sites across more than 200 countries, so a Display campaign can fill across niches you'd never reach through search alone. Campaigns get mature targeting, from affinity audiences to remarketing lists, plus brand safety controls like blocklists and content exclusions.

The advertiser side of this equation is Google Ads, not AdSense, so AdSense publishers don't control who advertises or at what price. If you're an advertiser reading this, the takeaway is simpler: AdSense inventory is one slice of the Display Network and it behaves like it, strongest for awareness and remarketing, weaker for tight bottom funnel campaigns.

## Google AdSense Approval Requirements

Consider this the pre-flight check, straight from [Google's program policies](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/48182){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} and practical field experience.

| Requirement | What Google expects |
|---|---|
| Content quality | Original, useful, substantial content. No scraped, spun, or low value pages |
| Content volume | No official minimum; 15 to 30 real pages beats thin filler |
| Essential pages | Privacy Policy, About, Contact, and Terms, reachable from every page |
| Site status | Live, publicly accessible, not under construction or password protected |
| Technical | HTTPS everywhere, mobile friendly, no mixed content |
| Compliance | AdSense Program Policies, Better Ads Standards, and consent management for EEA (IAB TCF v2.3 since March 2026) |
| Ownership | Full control of the site's HTML so you can place the ad code |
| Age | 18 or older; minors must apply through a parent or guardian |
| Domain age | No official minimum outside India and China; about six months expected in those regions |

You also need direct HTML access, and it's the requirement people tend to trip over. If your site hides the source from you, or a platform owns your code, AdSense can't even start. Sort that before you click apply.

## Key Takeaways

- AdSense remains the best starting network for new sites: free, fast to implement, and backed by the biggest advertiser pool.
- Publishers keep 80% of AdSense for Content revenue after the advertiser platform's fee, which nets out near 68% for Google Ads demand.
- The payout threshold is $100 USD, payment runs monthly between the 21st and 26th, and balances roll over until they clear it.
- Approval is the real bottleneck. Original content, essential pages, HTTPS, and HTML access decide most applications.
- Support is self serve only, and small niche sites should expect thin fill and modest CPMs until traffic grows.
- When you hit your traffic milestones, premium networks like Mediavine, AdThrive, and Raptive offer better economics and real human support.

## Conclusion

Google AdSense in 2026 is a trustworthy on-ramp. For most sites, though, it's not where you stay forever. Use it to learn the publisher side of ads, bank reliable monthly checks, and build the traffic that makes you interesting to better paying networks. Then move when the numbers justify it.
